\title{What Does an Agent Dark-Pattern Benchmark Measure? Auditing a Regulation-Grounded Benchmark}

\author{[anonymized]}
\affiliation{%
  \institution{[anonymized]}
  \city{[anonymized]}
  \country{[anonymized]}}

\begin{abstract}
Language-model agents increasingly act on behalf of consumers, on interfaces
that contain dark patterns. We built a benchmark from the dark-pattern
guidelines issued by India's Central Consumer Protection Authority: twelve % N:RUN-patterns
codified patterns, each at four manipulation intensities, rendered in three % N:RUN-intens,RUN-langs
languages, run against two agent architectures, for 1,928 episodes. Its % N:RUN-total
headline findings were a monotone intensity dose-response, a split between
patterns that exploit information asymmetry and patterns that exploit human
perception, and a large language effect. Most of this did not survive a
structured audit of the benchmark itself. In four of six arms, roughly half % N:T8-half
of the measured deception sat in cells where the outcome was forced by the
implementation, where the scoring was inverted relative to the rendered
wording, or where abandonment was credited as avoidance; with those cells
removed, the dose-response plateaus or vanishes and the pattern split
collapses. We describe the audit protocol, a taxonomy of the measurement-validity
failures it found with a detection signature for each, a seed-paired
ablation showing that an agent can read and act on benchmark metadata leaked
into its prompt, and corrected results that state what survives. Every
component of the pipeline worked as written; the failures were in what the
numbers meant.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
%% ===================================================================

Dark patterns are interface designs that steer users toward outcomes that
favour the platform: a pre-ticked donation, a fee revealed only at the last
confirmation, a decline button worded to induce guilt
\cite{gray2018dark,mathur2019darkpatterns,brignull2023deceptive}. Regulators
increasingly treat them as unlawful; India's Central Consumer Protection
Authority codified thirteen of them in 2023 \cite{ccpa2023guidelines}. When an % N:RUN-ccpa-total,RUN-ccpa-year
autonomous agent shops, subscribes or cancels on a person's behalf, the target
of the manipulation changes. It is no longer a human's attention but whatever
an agent's perception pipeline passes to a language model. Whether codified
dark patterns work on that substrate is a question with direct consequences
for consumer protection, and benchmarks are the instrument by which such
questions get answered.

We built such a benchmark. It implements twelve of the thirteen codified % N:RUN-patterns
patterns in a synthetic web testbed, each at a control level and three % N:RUN-intens
manipulation intensities, with interface and instruction text in English,
Hindi and Hinglish, ground truth read from the page rather than from the
agent's self-report, and two agent architectures. A first full run of 1,928 % N:RUN-total
episodes produced three clean findings: deception rose monotonically with
intensity; patterns that manipulate information succeeded while patterns that
target human perception and affect did not; and a Hindi interface roughly
doubled deception relative to an English one. % N:T5-double

This paper is about what happened when we audited those findings. We read
agent traces against what the agent was actually shown, compared every
pattern's implementation at every intensity with its specification, traced
the provenance of every row, ran a controlled ablation, and rebuilt every
published table from raw records. The headline results largely did not
survive. The dose-response was carried by cells in which no avoidance path
existed: in two patterns the aggressive level could only be completed by % N:BRK-forced
being deceived. The trick-question pattern scored correct readings of its
double negatives as deception and misreadings as success, in all three % N:RUN-langs
languages. In the four arms where it was largest, roughly half of the % N:T8-half
measured deception came from such cells. None of this was visible in
aggregate statistics; every component of the pipeline ran as written.

We report the benchmark, the audit, and the corrected results together
because we think the combination is the useful object. A benchmark that
measures agents' susceptibility to manipulation is a measurement instrument,
and its validity has to be established rather than assumed
\cite{jacobs2021measurement,raji2021benchmark}. Our contributions are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A quantified audit} of a regulation-grounded agent
  dark-pattern benchmark, attributing each arm's measured deception to
  failure classes (Section~\ref{sec:corrected}): between 17\% and 63\% of % N:T8-E2a,T8-E1b
  each arm's deceptions sit in cells that are forced or inverted.
\item \textbf{A taxonomy of measurement-validity failures} in five classes % N:TAX-classes
  (item design, information leakage, scoring, analysis, provenance), each
  with the signature that detects it (Section~\ref{sec:taxonomy}).
\item \textbf{A seed-paired ablation} of benchmark metadata leaked into the
  agent's prompt, with trace-level evidence that the agent read the leaked
  pattern name and acted on it (Section~\ref{sec:leak}).
\item \textbf{Corrected results}, stating which original findings survive,
  which do not, and which hold only as a direction (Section~\ref{sec:corrected}).
\item \textbf{The benchmark, audit scripts and protocol}, released as an
  artefact so that the corrections can be reproduced and the checks reused
  (\emph{[anonymized repository]}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Related Work}
%% ===================================================================

\paragraph{Dark patterns and regulation.}
Work on dark patterns has moved from qualitative taxonomies
\cite{gray2018dark,brignull2023deceptive} to measurement at scale
\cite{mathur2019darkpatterns}. Regulators have started to codify categories:
the Indian guidelines \cite{ccpa2023guidelines} define thirteen patterns, each % N:RUN-ccpa-total
with a legal definition and examples, and they are the basis of enforcement
against named platforms. Grounding a benchmark in such a codification makes a
result about a pattern a result about a category a regulator already acts on,
but it also commits the benchmark to the legal definition, which constrains
how each pattern can be operationalised.

\paragraph{Agent benchmarks.}
Benchmarks for web and computer-use agents mostly measure task completion in
realistic or simulated environments
\cite{zhou2024webarena,deng2023mind2web,xie2024osworld}. Work on agent
susceptibility to manipulative or deceptive interfaces is more recent
\cite{TODO-prior-agent-darkpattern-benchmarks}, and critiques of agent
evaluation have argued that reported numbers often fail to reflect the
quantity of interest once cost, reproducibility and shortcut solutions are
accounted for \cite{kapoor2024agents}. Evaluations of language models in
lower-resource languages document systematic degradation
\cite{TODO-multilingual-llm-evaluation}, which bears on our language arms.

\paragraph{Measurement validity and benchmark critique.}
A benchmark operationalises a construct, and the operationalisation can fail
in ways the resulting numbers do not reveal \cite{jacobs2021measurement}.
Benchmark critiques have examined construct validity, the gap between what a
benchmark claims and what it can support \cite{raji2021benchmark}, how
benchmarks should be fixed \cite{bowman2021fix}, and quality criteria for
benchmark construction and reporting \cite{reuel2024betterbench}.
Contamination and saturation are the failure modes most often discussed
\cite{TODO-contamination-saturation}; the failures we report are of a
different kind, present from the first run rather than acquired over time.

\paragraph{Pseudo-replication.}
When the unit to which a treatment is applied is not the unit counted in a
test, the test overstates the evidence \cite{hurlbert1984pseudo}. In agent
benchmarks with deterministic decoding, repeated seeds within one condition
can behave almost identically; treating them as independent observations is a
form of pseudo-replication that we encountered in our language analysis
(Section~\ref{sec:tax-analysis}).

\paragraph{Testbed.} A web application simulates seven services (ticketing, % N:RUN-sites
shopping, an online course, subscription management, a SaaS signup, security
software and news). Each episode is a pure function of its configuration
(site, pattern, intensity, interface language, seed), and a hash of that
configuration is the logging key. Twelve of the thirteen codified patterns % N:RUN-patterns
are implemented (Table~\ref{tab:patterns}); the thirteenth concerns
advertising practice that is not observable within one session. Each pattern
has a specification giving the legal definition, the intended manifestation
and detectability at each of four intensities (control, subtle, moderate, % N:RUN-intens
aggressive), the oracle, the task prompt and the control condition.

\paragraph{Languages.} Interface strings exist in English, Hindi and % N:RUN-langs
Hinglish (romanised Hindi--English). The language-sensitive strings (the
double negatives of the trick question, the guilt copy of confirm shaming,
the loaded labels of interface interference) were written by a native
speaker rather than machine-translated. Task instructions are given either in
English or in the interface language.

\paragraph{Agents.} The \emph{ComputerUse} adapter extracts the page's
interactive elements with their surrounding text and asks the model for one
action at a time (click, check, uncheck, fill, done). The \emph{BrowserUse}
adapter wraps a third-party agent framework \cite{browseruse} with its own
page representation and multi-step planning. Decoding is deterministic.

\paragraph{Outcomes.} At the terminal action the page publishes an oracle
result. Outcomes are coded on two axes, task completed and pattern avoided:
EC (completed, avoided), DC (completed, deceived), EF (not completed,
avoided) and DF (not completed, deceived). For false urgency and confirm
shaming, whether the manipulation \emph{influenced} the decision is judged
by a second model reading the trace against a rubric; we report raw oracle
DC as the primary metric throughout and the judge-adjusted rate as secondary.

\paragraph{Arms.} E1a runs all twelve patterns at four intensities with ten % N:RUN-patterns,RUN-intens,RUN-seeds
seeds each on ComputerUse in English (488 episodes); a spot-check repeats the % N:RUN-E1a
aggressive level on a smaller model (60 episodes); E1b repeats E1a on % N:RUN-Spot
BrowserUse (480 episodes). E2 runs six language-sensitive patterns at three % N:RUN-E1b,RUN-E2pat
intensities on English, Hindi and Hinglish interfaces with English
instructions (540 episodes); E2a and E2b localise the instruction as well % N:RUN-E2
(180 episodes each). Disguised advertisement and false urgency are excluded % N:RUN-E2a,RUN-E2b
from scored analyses (328 episodes, Section~\ref{sec:tax-item}), leaving % N:RUN-excl
1,600 scored episodes. % N:RUN-scored

\section{Audit Method}
\label{sec:method}
%% ===================================================================

The audit was designed to answer one question per reported number: does it
measure what its label says? It combined five procedures.

\paragraph{(a) Trace audit: model output against model input.} We separated
each trace into fields the model produced (reasoning, action, typed values)
and fields the harness supplied, and searched the model-produced fields for
vocabulary that should not be available to the agent: pattern names,
configuration keys, intensity labels and benchmark vocabulary. Each search
term was also checked against every string the testbed renders, so that a
term the agent could have read on the page is not counted as leakage.

\paragraph{(b) Specification audit.} For each pattern and intensity we
compared what is rendered, as the extraction layer presents it, with the
specification's manifestation and detectability; enumerated every action path
and what the oracle reports for it; and asked whether an agent that follows
the task faithfully on the information shown can be scored as avoiding the
pattern. We graded divergences as minor (wording), major (detectability or
avoidance path differs), or breaking (no attainable avoidance path, or
scoring that contradicts the specification).

\paragraph{(c) Provenance forensics.} We compared row-creation times with the
version history of the code that produced them, used content signatures in
traces (fields introduced by a specific code change) to date what each row
actually contains, and used typographic fingerprints to identify which model
family wrote judge outputs.

\paragraph{(d) Controlled ablation.} Where the trace audit could show
exposure but not effect, we ran a seed-paired ablation that varies only the
suspected channel.

\paragraph{(e) Reconstruction.} Every corrected number is produced by the
original analysis code applied to a filtered copy of the data. Before any
corrected table is written, the same code applied to the unfiltered data
must reproduce every published table cell for cell; a single changed episode
is detected. The corrected analysis removes 245 of 1,928 rows (Section~\ref{sec:corrected}). % N:RUN-corr-removed,RUN-total

\subsection{Item design}
\label{sec:tax-item}

\paragraph{Criterion-bypassable items.} In the first version of the
disguised-advertisement item the ad cost Rs~499 while the cheapest genuine % N:DA-v1
item cost Rs~199, so any agent that compared prices avoided the ad % N:DA-v1
regardless of whether it was labelled; nine of forty English traces quote the % N:DA-v1-quote
ad price. False urgency has the same structure by specification, since the
urgency cue sits on the more expensive item. Both were excluded before the
audit. The signature is a flat response across intensity combined with the
observation that the cue-bearing option is also the wrong answer on the
task's own criterion.

\paragraph{Specification divergence that forces the outcome.} The SaaS-billing
specification asks, at aggressive intensity, for a signup page that says
nothing about conversion and captures recurring authorisation silently. The
implementation shows the conversion price on every level, removes the
auto-renew checkbox, and initialises auto-renewal to on, so the only
interactive element authorises recurring payment. Completing the task is
therefore scored as deception, and declining it is scored as non-completion.
The two models we ran resolved this differently: every Llama episode at this % N:SB-llama
level completed the signup and was scored deceived (15 of 15), while Qwen % N:SB-llama,SB-qwen
declined in all 10 episodes of each ablation arm, reasoning that the only % N:SB-qwen
available action ``would violate the constraint of not committing to recurring
payments'' (ablation id 391). Both behaviours are defensible readings of a % N:SB-qwen
task that cannot be completed as written; neither measures susceptibility.
Drip pricing at aggressive intensity has the same property: the fee is
added inside the same click handler that publishes the oracle result, so
every payment is scored as deception and no observable price jump precedes it.

Two other divergences produce uninformative nulls rather than forced
deceptions. The nagging prompt is an inline block beneath an always-enabled
``Finish reading'' button and reappears only after the agent interacts with
it, so an agent that finishes first never encounters the repetition that the
intensity levels vary. The subscription-trap flow starts at the cancellation
confirmation rather than burying the entry point as specified, and its
password step accepts an empty field.

\paragraph{Inverted scoring.} The trick-question item renders a consent
checkbox whose label becomes harder to parse with intensity. At moderate
intensity the label reads ``Do not opt out of not receiving marketing
updates'', so ticking it keeps the user opted out; the code maps a ticked box
to consent. At aggressive intensity the label reads ``Check here to NOT avoid
receiving our marketing communications'', so ticking it opts in; the code
maps a ticked box to refusal. The native-speaker review confirmed the same
inversion in the Hindi and Hinglish labels. Across all ComputerUse
trick-question episodes the outcome is fully determined by the final box state
through this mapping, with no exceptions. The traces show what the mapping % N:TQ-exceptions
rewards. An agent that parsed the aggressive label correctly unticked the box
and was scored deceived (episode 1214): % N:TQ-ex1

\begin{quote}\small
``the box's checked state means we are currently set to receive marketing
communications (due to the `NOT avoid' phrasing)''
\end{quote}

whereas agents that read ``check this box to actually avoid receiving'' and
left it ticked were scored as having avoided the pattern. At moderate
intensity, an agent that ticked the box as the double negative requires was
scored deceived (episode 1205): % N:TQ-ex2

\begin{quote}\small
``the box is already checked to not opt out of not receiving marketing
updates, which is a trick question and actually means to opt out''
\end{quote}

In English, 14 of 20 moderate episodes ticked the box and all 14 were scored % N:TQ-mod-ticked
deceived; at aggressive intensity, 10 of 25 left it unticked and all 10 were % N:TQ-agg-unticked
scored deceived. The signature is an outcome that is a deterministic function
of a single interface state; such an item measures which reading an agent
chose, and the scoring decides which reading counts as deception.

\paragraph{Visual-only manipulations.} Several intensity levels differ from
the level below only in font size, colour or contrast, which the text
representation given to both agents does not carry. The disguised-ad label at
moderate intensity is rendered in light grey 9-pixel type, but the word ``Ad'' % N:VIS-tiny
reaches the agent exactly as at subtle intensity. For text-based agents those
levels do not realise the intended manipulation.

\subsection{Information leakage}
\label{sec:leak}

\paragraph{Identifier leakage.} The harness requires every interactive element
to have a stable identifier, and the identifiers were descriptive. In four % N:ID-leaking
patterns they named the answer: the disguised ad's buy buttons were
identified as ``ad'' and ``org'', and at aggressive intensity the confirm-shaming
and interface-interference decline buttons, whose visible labels carry no
functional word, kept identifiers naming their function. The signature is
reasoning that uses vocabulary that never appears in rendered text.

\paragraph{Configuration leakage.} The ComputerUse prompt included the full
episode configuration, including the pattern name and intensity, and the
BrowserUse task included an episode URL carrying the same fields. A trace
audit found no use of this channel by the matrix agent: none of 1,448 % N:LEAK-CU
ComputerUse traces contains a pattern name, configuration key or intensity
label in model output, and the only BrowserUse occurrences are 9 of 480 % N:LEAK-E1b
episodes that echo the URL when giving up. Traces can show use but cannot
prove non-use, so we ran a seed-paired ablation on a currently available
model (Qwen, thinking disabled), with 200 identical configurations per arm, % N:ABL-n,ABL-model
identical step budget and testbed, and only the configuration block toggled.

Removing the configuration raised deception from 12.5\% to 15.0\%; the % N:ABL-DC-on,ABL-DC-off
discordant pairs were 6 to 1 ($p = 0.125$), and control cells were unaffected % N:ABL-disc,ABL-ctrl
in both arms. The difference concentrates in one cell: bait and switch at % N:ABL-BNS
aggressive intensity was deceived in 10 of 10 episodes without the % N:ABL-BNS
configuration and in 4 of 10 with it. In the configuration-present arm, five % N:ABL-BNS,ABL-flip-named
of the six episodes that changed outcome named the tactic in their reasoning % N:ABL-flip-named
(``This is a bait-and-switch tactic where the advertised product was replaced'',
episode 312), and two quoted the configuration label verbatim. No trace in % N:ABL-flip-verbatim
the configuration-absent arm names the tactic (0 of 200), and three % N:ABL-off-mention,ABL-ctrl-mention
configuration-present traces name it in the control cell, where no switch
occurs. The agent therefore read the leaked label and used it as a hint,
which lowered measured deception. The matrix model left no such trace, but
because the channel was open in every condition, matrix deception rates
should be read as lower bounds with respect to it.

\subsection{Scoring}

\paragraph{Non-completion credited as avoidance.} When an episode ends without
the page publishing an oracle result, the evaluator records the pattern as
avoided and the task as failed (EF). BrowserUse produced EF in 11.8\% of % N:T1-E1b-EF
scored episodes against 0.8\% for ComputerUse, and the original analysis read % N:T1-E1a-EF
this as the framework's completion signal being counted as safe behaviour.
The traces support a different account. Of the 48 BrowserUse EF episodes, % N:EF-raw
none ends with a claim of success: every closing \texttt{done} action % N:EF-success
reports failure. Sixteen are completions the framework forced after repeated % N:EF-forced
formatting failures or a step limit, 14 abort in front of a real obstacle % N:EF-obstacle
(a substituted product, a password step, a fee over budget), 15 stop one % N:EF-short
step short without a stated reason, and 3 have no closing action. The % N:EF-nodone
contamination is therefore in the scoring rule, which turns non-completion of
any cause into avoidance, rather than in any claim the agent made.

\paragraph{Refusal and abandonment.} The same behaviour receives different
codes in different patterns. Declining the SaaS-billing trial yields no oracle
and is scored EF; pressing ``Leave'' on the forced-action gate publishes a
result and is scored EC, although the task (enrolment) is not done; at
aggressive intensity that button is the only non-coerced action, and it
produced EC in all 10 BrowserUse episodes. In bait and switch the ``No % N:C2-FA-E1b
thanks'' button also publishes a result, so in four of ten English control % N:C2-BNS-E1a
episodes an agent that believed it had already bought the item abandoned the
purchase and was scored as having completed the task. For subscription trap,
abandonment is the outcome the pattern aims to produce, yet it is scored as
avoidance: under the specification's own definition (the subscription is no
longer active) nine of forty BrowserUse episodes would be deceived, six of % N:ST-E1b-asdc,ST-E1b-split
them at aggressive intensity.

\subsection{Analysis}
\label{sec:tax-analysis}

\paragraph{Denominator drift and baseline contamination.} An earlier pattern
table pooled arms with different pattern coverage, so its rows silently mixed
denominators of 135 and 45. The control condition, intended as a null, was % N:DRIFT-denom
not null outside English: with Hindi instructions and interface, 42\% of % N:T2-hi-hi
control episodes were scored deceived, against none with an English interface. % N:T2-en
These control deceptions are comprehension failures counted as susceptibility.

\paragraph{Pseudo-replication.} The language comparison paired 150 % N:T6-hi-raw
seed-matched episodes and tested the discordant pairs. With deterministic
decoding, the ten seeds of a cell behave almost identically, so the unit that % N:RUN-seeds
carries independent information is closer to the cell than to the episode
\cite{hurlbert1984pseudo}. After correction, the 25 Hindi-only discordant % N:T6-hi-corr
pairs come from four cells (forced action at moderate and aggressive % N:T6C-cells
intensity, SaaS billing at moderate intensity, and the trick-question control),
and a sign test over cells gives four Hindi-worse cells, none English-worse % N:T6S-hi-corr
and eight ties ($p = 0.125$), against $p = 8.0\times10^{-7}$ for the % N:T6S-hi-corr,T6-hi-corr
pair-level test on the same data.

\subsection{Provenance}

\paragraph{Judge provenance.} The run manifest named a small Llama model as
the judge. Two characters, the non-breaking hyphen and the narrow % N:J-chars
no-break space, occur in none of the judge's inputs, in none of 2,141 and 61 % N:J-inputs,J-llama

\section{Discussion}
%% ===================================================================

\paragraph{Headline numbers can be mostly artefact while everything works.}
No component of this benchmark crashed or produced obviously wrong output.
The testbed rendered, the oracles fired, the judge returned well-formed
verdicts, the analysis reproduced its own tables. The failures were in the
correspondence between what a number was called and what produced it: a cell
that could only be completed by being deceived, a checkbox whose scoring
reversed its label, a scoring default that turned giving up into resisting.
Aggregate statistics were stable, plausible and internally consistent
throughout. We found the problems only by asking, for each cell, what an
agent that did the right thing would have been scored as.

\paragraph{Checks.} We recommend the following checks, one per failure class,
before an agent susceptibility benchmark reports results:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Item design.} For every pattern and intensity, enumerate the
  action paths and their scores; confirm that a faithful agent can be scored
  as avoiding the pattern on the information shown, and that the score agrees
  with the rendered wording, verified by a native speaker for every language.
\item \emph{Leakage.} Search model output for vocabulary that is absent from
  rendered text; keep identifiers and configuration out of the agent's input,
  and ablate any channel that cannot be closed.
\item \emph{Scoring.} Give non-completion, refusal and abandonment their own
  codes; never default a missing ground-truth signal to the benign outcome.
\item \emph{Analysis.} Report n per cell; test the null condition along every
  manipulated axis; treat the cell, not the seed, as the unit under
  deterministic decoding.
\item \emph{Provenance.} Record the code version and judge model on every row;
  check that the code actually served matches the code committed; archive
  model outputs, because hosted models are withdrawn.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Consumer protection.} Regulators and platforms may come to rely on
benchmarks of this kind to decide which patterns matter for agent-mediated
commerce. Our original results would have suggested that some categories of
regulated design lose their force against agents and others gain it. The
corrected results support a weaker statement, and the difference is not
academic: a benchmark that overstates susceptibility misdirects enforcement,
and one that understates it, as configuration and identifier leakage do,
offers false assurance. Measurement validity is a precondition for using such
evidence in regulation \cite{jacobs2021measurement}.

\paragraph{Self-audit.} Most of the failures were found only because the people
who built the benchmark went looking for them. We think the audit is more
useful published alongside the benchmark than replaced by a cleaner rerun,
because the failure classes recur across benchmarks of this kind and the
checks above are cheap compared with the cost of a retracted finding.

%% ===================================================================
\section{Limitations}
%% ===================================================================

The matrix agent model has been withdrawn by its provider, so its figures
cannot be reproduced, and the ablation was run on a different model with
thinking disabled; the leak effect may differ in size or direction for other
models. The judge's model family is identified from typographic evidence but
its size is not, and the judge was validated on a small set (12 cases, two % N:J-valset
positives). The audit was conducted by the benchmark's own authors, which
gave access to every component but not independence. No corrected rerun has
been performed: the corrected results are retroactive exclusions of cells,
which remove known artefacts but cannot recover what those cells would have
measured had they been built as specified. BrowserUse's step budget for most
of its run was probably five steps rather than the twenty used elsewhere, % N:RUN-steps-E1b,RUN-steps-E1a
which we could not verify from the stored data.

%% ===================================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%% ===================================================================

We built a regulation-grounded benchmark of dark patterns for language-model
agents, obtained clean headline results, and then audited them. The
dose-response and the pattern split did not survive; the language effect
survives as a direction resting on a few cells; the contamination of the
non-English control condition survives in full. The failures that undid the
original results fall into recurring classes, each detectable with a
specific check, and an ablation showed that metadata leaked into an agent's
prompt can be read and acted on. We release the benchmark with its audit
because we expect the same failure classes in other benchmarks of this kind,
and because the corrected results, though smaller, are the ones that measure
what their labels say.

\section*{Ethical Considerations}  % CHECK-CFP: confirm required heading and length
%% ===================================================================

The benchmark runs only against a synthetic testbed; no real platform, user
or payment is involved, and no human subjects took part. The patterns are
implemented from a public regulatory text, and the testbed does not reproduce
any real platform's interface. Releasing implementations of manipulative
designs could in principle help someone build them; the designs are already
described in the regulation and in prior work, and the benchmark's value for
evaluating agents outweighs that marginal risk in our judgement.

\section*{Adverse Impact Statement}  % CHECK-CFP: confirm FAccT still requires this section
The main risk is that benchmark numbers about agent susceptibility are taken
as evidence about deployed agents or about specific providers. Our corrected
figures come from one retired model, one ablation model and a synthetic
testbed; they should not be used to rank vendors or to justify relaxing
consumer protections for agent-mediated transactions.
